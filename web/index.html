§01Problem definition

A score that measures spending, used as if it measured sickness

  1. The programme

    Hospitals give their highest-risk patients extra nursing, priority appointments and follow-up. A commercial score ranks everyone: the top 3% are auto-identified and the top 45% are referred to their doctor. Tools of this class score ~200 million people a year in the US.

  2. The proxy

    The score predicts next year's medical cost and treats it as a stand-in for health need. Race is never an input.

  3. The harm

    At the same level of illness, less is spent on Black patients because of unequal access. An accurate cost model therefore ranks them as healthier than they are, and they are under-referred.

§03Background & context

Seven years from discovery: more rules, few that bite

2019

Bias published in Science; New York regulators and US senators demand answers.

Fix exists

The vendor's own re-test on 3.7 M patients cut the bias 84% with a combined health-and-cost label.

2022

A bug fix in the authors' code makes the counterfactual finding stronger (47% → 59%).

2024–26

New AI rules in all three jurisdictions, but several were delayed, narrowed or repealed before they could apply.

    §09Conclusion

    Detection was never the hard part. The obligation to look was.

    Limitations

    • The cohort is synthetic: it preserves structure, not exact magnitudes. It has no patient IDs, so intervals treat patient-years as independent.
    • Chronic-condition counts are themselves shaped by access to care, so bias estimates are lower bounds.
    • "White" in the source data means all non-Black patients. Race is treated as a social category acting through access, not biology.
    • The legal coding is our reading of primary sources as of 7 October 2026, not legal advice. HTI-5, the Colorado litigation and the GDPR omnibus were still in motion.
